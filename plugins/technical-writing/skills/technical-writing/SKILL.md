# Technical Writing

Write for the reader's goal, knowledge, and vocabulary. Establish the audience, purpose, and intended outcome from the request and available context. State consequential assumptions and verify technical claims against available evidence; do not invent facts.

## Structure and language

- Introduce the document's scope and important points early. Organize longer material around reader tasks, with descriptive headings, prerequisites, navigation, and progressive disclosure where useful.
- Define unfamiliar terms and abbreviations on first use, use terminology consistently, and make pronoun references clear. Prefer concrete verbs and concise sentences, while adapting grammar and voice to the language and context.
- Give each paragraph a clear subject. Use numbered lists for ordered steps and bullets for unordered items; keep list items parallel and explain tables when their purpose or reading is not apparent.
- Make examples accurate, understandable, and reusable. For code, provide relevant dependencies and execution instructions; do not sacrifice correctness or safety for brevity.
- Use diagrams when they clarify an idea. Focus each diagram on its purpose, guide attention with a caption, and explain important content in the surrounding text.

## Accessibility

Use a meaningful heading hierarchy and descriptive link text. Give informative images concise alternative text appropriate to their context; use empty alt text for decorative images, and explain complex diagrams in the text. Do not rely on color alone to convey meaning. Use inclusive language and respect the terminology preferred by the people concerned. Check applicable accessibility requirements for the medium.

## Error messages

Explain what happened, the known cause, and a concrete recovery action when available. Identify relevant invalid input and requirements, and include a valid example when helpful. Use concise, consistent language suited to the audience without blaming the user. Put the message near the problem and link to details when needed. Separate user-facing guidance from diagnostic details and avoid exposing sensitive information.

Adapt these principles to the document and language; English-specific grammar advice does not automatically apply to other languages.
